Safe Route Planner

Most map apps pick the fastest route. This one picks the safest one first, then the one that uses the least fuel, then the quickest. It counts what makes a drive risky: turns across traffic at busy or crash-prone junctions, school zones, railroad crossings, closures and driving into a low sun, and it shows what the safer route avoids compared with the standard one.

It plans car, bike, walking and bus trips around Milwaukee on its own router over OpenStreetMap roads, using public crash data and work zones.

Try a trip:

  • Fiserv Forum → State Fair ParkBy car: the safer route scores 100 for safety against the standard route's 26, for about 7 more minutes.
  • UW-Milwaukee → Bay ViewBy car: the safer route scores 100 for safety against the standard route's 37, for about 9 more minutes.
  • Bayshore → Fiserv ForumBy bike: the lowest-stress streets and trails, with busy crossings marked.
  • Fiserv Forum → Bay ViewBy bus: walk, ride and walk, with the safest option first.

This copy runs without its server: routes between 12 sample places were recorded from the real engine, so points you set move to the nearest sample place. The map, layers, directions and the 3D drive preview run live in your browser.

    How it works

    Most navigation apps pick the fastest route. This one puts your safety first, then fuel, then time: of the routes it finds, the safest wins; among equally safe ones, the one that burns the least fuel; then the fastest.

    What makes a route risky

    • Turns across oncoming traffic at busy junctions, less where a traffic signal controls them, more where crashes have happened.
    • Closed roads and lane closures, live from Wisconsin's work zones and 511, and TomTom traffic.
    • School zones at drop-off and pickup times, and railroad crossings.
    • Sun glare: driving toward a low sun around sunrise and sunset, worked out from where the sun is when you leave.
    • After dark: turns across traffic count as riskier: oncoming cars are judged by their headlights.
    • Weather: National Weather Service alerts, and fog, ice or flooding reported on the roads. On snow and ice, turns across traffic count as riskier.

    Each route gets a safety score from 0 to 100 that adds these up the same way for everyone: higher is safer.

    How the routes are found

    Our own router searches Milwaukee's streets for routes that avoid those risks, then compares each with the standard, fastest route. You choose how much extra time a safer route may take, and when you leave: school zones and the sun change with the time.
